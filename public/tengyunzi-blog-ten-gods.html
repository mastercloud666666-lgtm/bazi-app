The Ten Gods in BaZi

Once you know your Day Master, everything else in the chart is read through how it relates to you. That set of relationships is called the Ten Gods, and it is where interpretation really begins.

  • Foundation
  • Ten Gods
  • 8 min read

What the Ten Gods Actually Are

The Ten Gods (Shi Shen) are not deities and not omens. They are relationship labels. Each of the other elements in your chart is described by one thing only: how it stands in relation to your Day Master.

There are five possible relationships, and each splits into two by yin-yang polarity. Five times two is ten. That is the entire system.

The Five Relationships

Everything starts from the generating and controlling cycles of the Five Elements, measured from your Day Master outward.

Why Each Splits Into Two

When the related element shares your polarity, the relationship reads as steadier and more conventional. When the polarity differs, it reads as sharper and more unconventional.

This is why the classic texts pair a “proper” and an “eccentric” version of each god — Proper Officer and Seven Killings, Proper Wealth and Indirect Wealth, and so on. Same relationship, different temperature.

How the Ten Gods Shape a Reading

Read together, the Ten Gods describe how you tend to engage with work, expression, support, structure, and other people. A chart heavy in Output leans expressive; one heavy in Officer leans toward duty and structure.

Notice the framing: these are tendencies in temperament, not forecasts of events. The Ten Gods tell you how you are wired to relate, not what is going to happen to you.

The Ten Gods are relationships, not fortunes. They describe how you tend to engage — not what the future holds.
