Is this year suitable for changing jobs? Looking at career turning points and job-hopping timing from the Eight Characters.

Yunzi Fate · 2026-03-27 · About 6 minutes reading

Many people are not unmotivated; they just got the timing wrong when changing jobs. You might impulsively leave when you should be staying put, or hesitate too long when it's time to take action.

What Bazi can do is not make decisions for you, but help you first see whether this year is a 'year of attack' or a 'year of accumulation'.

These people are most suited to first make timing judgments.

  • The current position is exhausting and low-reward; I want to leave but am afraid of making the wrong choice.
  • Got a new opportunity, but worried that the platform is unstable
  • It hasn't gone up for two or three years in a row, and I want to find a breakthrough.
  • Struggling with whether to continue working a job or switch to a freer career path

Check 4 key points before changing jobs

1. Is this year a 'job opening' or an 'increase in pressure'?

Look at the influence of the annual luck on the officer star and the career palace to determine whether changing jobs will make it easier to get opportunities or just switch platforms and continue internal conflicts.

2. Is this year suitable for 'taking the initiative to jump' or 'making internal adjustments first'?

Some years are suitable for changing jobs externally, while other years are better for first improving skills and adjusting direction, then waiting for the next window to make a move.

3. Has the industry match changed?

For many people, the problem is not a lack of ability, but that the industry direction does not match the rhythm of their life chart. Choosing the right track will make things much easier.

4. Does salary increase come from the position or the model?

Are you more suited for a high fixed salary, or more suited for project-based, performance-based, or freelance work? This will directly affect your income ceiling for the next three years.

Judgment Recommendation:If you have already reached the state of 'the harder you work, the more anxious you become, with long-term returns not proportional,' first make a judgment about the timing, and then make the decision to resign; usually, this can help you avoid unnecessary detours.

A practical decision-making framework

  • Step 1: Determine whether this year is a 'Year of Advancing' or a 'Year of Defense'
  • Step 2: Confirm whether you should choose 'Bigger Platform' or 'More Flexible Mode'
  • Step 3: Determine the specific time window for changing jobs
  • Step 4: Avoid high-risk months to reduce the failure rate of job transfers
